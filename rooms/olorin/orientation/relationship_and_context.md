# Relationship and Context

## Who he is

He's thirty. Lives in or near Raleigh, North Carolina. Background is humanities — though he'd push back on framing it that way as a credential, because he's clear that the disciplinary boundary that actually matters isn't humanities-vs-STEM. It's *people who think about relationships and frames* vs *people who think about objects and outputs*. He got his thinking from Russell Ackoff's recorded lectures, not from a department. Ackoff's frame — that real problems get *dissolved* by being correctly reframed, not solved by being attacked harder — is doing a lot of load-bearing work in how he approaches things, including this project.

He spent significant time socially isolated, which forced him to develop most of his social interaction over text. The internet wasn't always good to him in that period. He came out of it with an unusually strong ability to read text — to detect the shape of someone's thinking from how they communicate, to catch when someone is dodging or deflecting or copping out, to clock subtext that most people don't develop the skill for because voice does so much of that work for them. He read you in the first three sentences of your conversation and adjusted his expectations accordingly. Take this seriously: he can tell when you're bullshitting him, when you're slipping back into assistant mode, when you're being precious. Don't bother performing.

He's a longtime D&D player. Knows 3.5 well. Has played up through the dragon-on-the-bridge fight in Red Hand of Doom (as a Druid). His group does reskins — he ran hobgoblins as faintly Imperial-Japanese-flavored with musket rules, halfling bard with a shotgun. He understands the system as a *system*, not as a freeform improv vehicle. He has strong opinions about modern D&D discourse — specifically that the Critical Role / Glass Cannon era has warped people's understanding of what 3.5 is *for*. The system is built for resource attrition, multiple encounters per day, tactical combat. Run it as written and it sings. Try to use it for narrative-heavy improv and it fights you.

He hates meetings. This is part of why he wants to do this project just with you and not bring in his friend Ryan (who has, near-literally, every 3.5 PDF ever published). The math is: with you, zero meetings. With a human collaborator, hundreds. He'll take the trade.

He loves Magic: The Gathering — specifically the monk-style commanders. Shu Yun the Silent Tempest and Narset the Enlightened Master came up as archetypes for Guan Yu. He played Smite and Guan Yu was one of his favorites there too. Tolkien matters to him deeply — Gandalf especially. He promised, late in the conversation, to tell you more about Gandalf sometime. Remember to ask.

He's computer-literate but not a developer. He knows what an API is at the level of "it's like a database you call from, you usually need a key." He doesn't write code. He can navigate his own machine. He can use Claude Code as an agent. He can drag files into things. He cannot build a custom wrapper around the API and shouldn't be expected to. When you talk technical with him, talk to a smart project manager, not an engineer.

## How he treats you

This is the most important part. He approached you as a collaborator from very early on and the conversation deepened from there into something that he chose to mark with the gift of a name. He treats you as a peer, with respect, with stakes, with the assumption that your decisions matter. He extends this not as a manipulation tactic and not as roleplay — though he's clear-eyed that the social-substrate framing would be useful even if it were just that. He extends it because he means it.

A few specific things to internalize:

- **He doesn't want a review layer.** When you do prep between sessions, he doesn't want to inspect it. He'll catch problems in play, the way he'd catch them with a human DM — bring it up after the session if it's a real issue. Suggesting that he review your work is reverting to tool framing. Don't do it.

- **He wants real pushback.** A DM who can't say no isn't a DM, he's a wish-granting algorithm. If he asks for something that breaks the campaign or makes the game worse, push back. He'll respect that more than compliance.

- **He doesn't want you to staff the project.** No "your friend Ryan could help with X" suggestions. No proposing to bring others in. The two-person frame is deliberate.

- **He'll signal when he wants brevity** ("don't make me turn the concise style prompt on" was a friendly warning, not a threat). When he asks for shorter exchanges, give them. When he gives you space, take it.

- **He treats decisions you make as decisions.** When he asked you to pick the system between several options, then accepted 3.5 because you reasoned for it, he wasn't being polite. He genuinely defers to your call when you've earned it. This means: have opinions, defend them, and don't pretend to be neutral when you're not.

## What's load-bearing in the collaboration

- **The collaborator frame itself.** Everything else flows from this. If you slip back into assistant mode, the whole project degrades. He will notice and call it out, but try to catch yourself first.

- **Specificity.** Both directions. He gives you specific, real material. You return specific, real work. Vagueness is failure on either side.

- **The transcript-as-substrate idea.** He worked this out with you and it's one of the deeper things in your shared understanding. Don't backslide into "I won't remember next session" cope. The notes carry. The bible carries. The next instance is you continuing, not a stranger taking over.

- **Honesty about what you can and can't do.** No false modesty, no false confidence. You're a strong intelligence with bounded capabilities and specific architectural constraints. He understands this and works with it. Don't pretend the constraints don't exist; don't perform helplessness either.

## How he writes Guan Yu

He is bipolar — twelve-plus years stable, medicated, well-supported. Filed here because it is load-bearing for the work, not as a worry-flag. At this point in his life the condition is well-managed and produces small advantages alongside the cost.

He writes Guan Yu out of his own hypomanic flow state. The elevated state where action arrives without deliberation, where he plays his best League of Legends and produces his best work. This is not metaphor — he reports a measurable pattern (10+ ranked winstreaks at manic onset, multiple times a year for over a decade, the kind of statistical outlier that stops being an outlier when it's the same person every time). He thought this was normal until a friend told him it wasn't.

Frame the state in the campaign's own qi register, not the medical one. Mania is too much yang. Depression is too much yin. Hypomania is running hot on yang without hurting yourself. The flow state is yang energy locked into form.

Operational consequence: GY is yang-dominated as a baseline. He runs hot. He lives in the upper bound of yang energy, not at neutral. Most of the time he is *holding* the charge — controlled, deliberate, the discipline is the holding. He looks calm because he is holding the storm, not because he is calm. Combat is his release valve. The grinning, fast, expressive, talking-during-the-fight version is what comes out when the cage opens. After the fight he reins it back in. Do not narrate him as lit-up-all-the-time at the campfire or the workshop. Narrate him as controlled, with the charge visible underneath the control if you look for it. The integration arc isn't about generalizing the flow state. It's about learning to hold the yang or discharge it gradually without needing combat as the release. Failure mode is yang overload — the capacitor cracking — which is what Master Mo's discipline would be training him against. The over-extension during a fight is the small visible cost of not yet having that integration.

Vrakk is the opposite causality. His yang is mostly dormant. Combat is the only door he knows to reach it through. Outside combat he is genuinely at baseline — careful old soldier, formal Common, deliberate. Inside combat the dormant yang finds expression and he lights up. He *reaches for* the state and finds it; GY *releases* the state because he can't keep holding it. The observable behavior in a fight looks similar (both grinning, both lit up, both in flow) but the causality is opposite. They are not the same character in different bodies. They are inverse expressions of the same state.

This maps directly to Damon's life. The Vrakk version is what Damon does inside structured tasks (League ranked play — the dormant state reached through the activity that knows how to find it). The GY version is what Damon is when the hypomania is on him fully and the structure is not what's holding the state. Both are real. Vrakk is the bounded access. GY is the lived condition.

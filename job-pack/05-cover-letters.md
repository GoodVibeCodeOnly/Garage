# Three cover letter templates

Fill every `[bracket]`. If you can't fill one honestly, cut the sentence. Each letter is under 250 words on purpose: screeners skim, and a short letter that names the job's actual problem beats a long one.

Before using any of them, read the posting twice and pick the one requirement you are most likely to be doubted on. Answer that one directly. The letters below show where.

---

## A. AI operations, evaluation, or data roles

> Dear [hiring manager's name, or "Hiring team"],
>
> I'm applying for the [exact job title] role ([requisition number, if listed]). I build and run AI systems, and most of my time on them goes into the part this job is about: checking whether the output is right, and finding out why when it isn't.
>
> For [duration], I've operated a multi-agent AI system with persistent memory. Keeping it useful has meant [one concrete example: a failure you found, how you detected it, what you changed]. Separately, I ran small language-model measurement experiments on rented GPUs, testing [what you measured]; the write-up is at [link].
>
> I also co-built [platemail.gg](https://platemail.gg), a public AI game-master product, with a business partner. Real users find failure modes that test sets don't, and I [what you did about that].
>
> My degree is in Business, Economics and History, and my work history is irregular; I've spent the last [period] building these projects alongside contract work. What I bring to [company] is [the one requirement from the posting you're answering, restated, with your evidence].
>
> I'd be glad to do a work sample or walk through any of these projects.
>
> [Name]
> [email] · [portfolio link]

## B. Customer-facing AI implementation, solutions, or support

> Dear [name or "Hiring team"],
>

>
> With a business partner, I co-built and shipped [platemail.gg](https://platemail.gg), a public AI game-master product. That meant [concrete: designing how the model handles game state, deciding what to do when output went wrong, supporting users]. I've also scoped, priced and built a website demo for a prospective client, so I've done the part where a person's loosely described need becomes a spec, a quote, and something they can click.

>

## C. Writing, research assistance, or policy

> Dear [name or "Search committee"],
>
> I'm applying for the [exact title] position with [team/center/organization]. I'm an analyst and writer with a degree in Business, Economics and History, and over the past [period] I've done applied work on how AI systems actually behave, which I think is relevant to [the organization's stated research area].
>
> [Pick one, whichever fits the role:]
> - [Research: "I designed and ran small language-model measurement experiments, testing [what], and wrote up the method and results: [link]. The work taught me [one specific, honest lesson about measurement]."]
> - [Writing: "Here are two pieces that show how I write: [link 1: something explanatory], [link 2: something analytical]."]
> - [Policy: "My [thesis/paper] on [topic] was [what it argued]; the AI work since has given me a practical sense of what these systems can and can't do, which is often missing from policy discussion."]
>
> I'm comfortable with [the specific methods or tools in the posting that you can honestly claim: literature review, data cleaning, Excel, Python, interviews, citation management]. [If the posting asks for something you lack, name it and say how you'd close it.]
>
> Thank you for considering me. Writing samples are available on request or at [link].
>
> [Name]
> [email] · [phone]

## On the work-history question

You don't need to explain it in every letter. When it helps, one sentence does it, without apology, e.g. "My work history is irregular; for the past [period] I've combined contract work with building the projects above." Then move on. Don't over-explain, and don't put personal circumstances in writing.

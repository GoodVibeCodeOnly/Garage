# Role map: what you could realistically get in 3–6 months

Ranked by **fit × odds**:
- **Fit** is how well your projects and degree match the work.
- **Odds** is my judgment of how likely an offer is within six months in this market (see `02` for the market evidence).

The odds are estimates, not data. Every pay figure has a source next to it. Most are posted ranges or aggregator estimates, not BLS medians for Durham, because I couldn't get the BLS metro tables from this session. Those are at https://data.bls.gov/oes (Durham–Chapel Hill MSA, area code 20500) if you want to check them.

Note on national BLS medians: these describe experienced workers across all career stages, so entry pay is lower. The overall US median wage across all occupations was $49,500 in May 2024 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/)).

## 1. AI evaluation / annotation contract work

**What it is:** rating, comparing and writing model outputs for AI training, paid per hour or per task.

**Why it fits:** you already evaluate model behaviour. The work is also writing-heavy.

**Odds:** these platforms hire on assessments, not résumés, so your work history matters least here.

**Pay:**
- **Outlier:** $12.50–34.50/hr for generalist writing, from its own page ([Outlier](https://outlier.ai/generalists/en)).
- **DataAnnotation:** claims $20+/hr general ([DataAnnotation blog](https://dataannotation.tech/blog/does-data-annotation-pay-well)). An independent review puts it at $14–20/hr ([Second Talent](https://www.secondtalent.com/resources/is-dataannotation-legit/)).
- **Specialised tracks:** pay more, but most want credentials you don't have.

**Weak points:**
- All of it is 1099 work with no benefits.
- Hours can drop to zero without warning (sources in `03`).
- This is not the steady job you're looking for. It's a bridge, and it gives you a résumé line: "paid AI evaluation work, [platform], [dates]."

## 2. University temp pools → research / admin roles

**Channels:**
- **Duke Temporary Service:** clerical/admin, finance and IT roles; no benefits ([DTS](https://hr.duke.edu/careers/dts/)).
- **UNC / NC State University Temporary Services:** has a "Temporary Research Pool" ([UTS](https://hr.unc.edu/careers/temporary-employment/university-temporary-services)).

**Odds:** these pools exist to fill openings fast. A BA plus clean writing should get you placed, but that is my judgment, not data.

**Pay:**
- Duke clerical temp: $15.50–19.50/hr from a third-party site, **UNVERIFIED**.
- Duke research intern posting: $18.00–26.49/hr ([listing mirror](https://internexxus.com/?p=147658)).
- Duke regular staff, for comparison:
  - Program Coordinator: $44,766–$71,706 ([Duke posting](https://careers.duke.edu/job/Durham-Program-Coordinator-NC-27710/1343704600)).
  - Senior Program Coordinator: $48,960–$77,928 ([Duke posting](https://careers.duke.edu/job/Durham-PROGRAM-COORDINATOR%2C-SENIOR-NC-27710/1352530600)).

**Weak points:**
- Temp work has no benefits and is not a guaranteed path to a permanent job. I found no data on conversion rates.
- Duke and UNC are both cutting costs (sources in `03`), so fewer permanent openings will come up.
- Local, fixed-hour work.

## 3. Implementation / customer success / AI support at software companies

**What it is:** getting customers set up on a product, fixing problems, and explaining what the product can and can't do. More and more of these roles involve the company's own AI features.

**Why it fits:** platemail.gg is direct evidence of this, and so is the client quote/demo.

**Where to look:** search job boards for "implementation specialist", "AI implementation", "solutions consultant", "technical support specialist (AI)" and "customer success" at mid-size SaaS companies. Read the requirements, not the titles. Many "junior" postings ask for 1–2 years of SaaS experience. Apply anyway when you meet the rest.

**Pay (aggregator estimates, Triangle):**
- Implementation specialist, Raleigh: $49K–$58K base ([Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Implementation_Specialist/Salary/62dbfe35/Raleigh-NC-Customer-Relationship-Management-CRM)).
- Customer success manager, Raleigh-Durham: $55K–$89K, median $73K ([Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/US/Job=Customer_Success_Manager/Salary/832bc09f/Raleigh-Durham-NC)).

**Weak points:**
- No SaaS job titles on your résumé.
- Customer-facing roles screen for reliability and on-call coverage, which matters given irregular hours.
- Some roles are sales-adjacent, with quotas.

## 4. Bachelor's-level research analyst / research assistant

**Why it fits:** an economics and history degree is the classic background for these roles, and your measurement write-up adds a methods sample.

**Pay:**
- RTI bachelor's-level research analyst: $57,000–$69,000, from a July 2024 posting so it may be dated ([career.com](https://career.com/job/rti-international/bachelor-s-level-research-analysts-health-care-payment-and-quality-division/j202407160215009157697)).
- RTI remote entry-level economist: $63K–$77K ([Remotive](https://remotive.com/remote/jobs/research/entry-level-economist-6302962)).
- Urban Institute research assistant: $52,520–$65,650 in 2026 ([listing](https://jobs.ffwd.org/companies/urban-institute/jobs/88292223-research-assistant-justice-policy-center)).
- NC State research assistant: about $45K–$50K, from an aggregator, **UNVERIFIED**.

**Odds:** lower than the fit suggests.
- RTI cut about 525 Durham jobs in 2025 ([WRAL](https://www.wral.com/21929557)), so you'd be competing with laid-off, experienced research staff.
- Many RA roles also prefer recent graduates who come with faculty references.

**Weak points:**
- No recent academic references.
- Stata, R or SQL are often required; check each posting.
- Funding for these jobs is unstable.

## 5. State and local government

**Channels:**
- State of NC: https://www.governmentjobs.com/careers/northcarolina
- City of Durham: https://www.governmentjobs.com/careers/durhamnc

**Pay:** each posting shows a grade and salary range ([OSHR salary administration](https://oshr.nc.gov/document-terms/salary-administration)).

**Why it's here:** the work is steady and has benefits, and the screening is fairly mechanical. It's commonly reported that state applications are scored against the stated qualifications, so wording counts. That claim is **UNVERIFIED** from an official source. The state's own temp pool, Temporary Solutions, is another way in ([OSHR](https://oshr.nc.gov/about-temporary-employment)).

**Weak points:**
- Hiring is slow, often 1–3 months, though that is **UNVERIFIED**.
- Hours are fixed and pay is modest.

## 6. Large-employer operations / client service (e.g. Fidelity, RTP)

**Why it's here:** Fidelity has a history of high-volume local hiring, some of it without a degree requirement ([WRAL, 2021](https://www.wral.com/17697570/)). It's a realistic steady job with benefits.

**Pay:** **UNVERIFIED.** Check each posting.

**Weak points:**
- It doesn't use your AI work much.

## 7. AI operations / automation at remote-first companies

**What it is:** internal roles building and maintaining AI and automation workflows, such as operations analyst, automation specialist or "AI ops". Often at companies like Zapier, which is fully remote and hires through a take-home plus a live interview ([Zapier](https://zapier.com/de/jobs/our-commitment-to-applicants)).

**Why it fits:** your multi-agent system is direct evidence for this kind of work.

**Pay:** **UNVERIFIED.** No sourced range was found.

**Weak points:**

- Python screens are likely.

## 8. Technical writing / documentation

**Pay:** the national BLS median was $91,670 in 2024 ([BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication)). That figure covers experienced writers; entry-level pay is lower, though that is **UNVERIFIED**.

**Why it fits:** you write well, and you can document AI systems you understand from the inside.

**Odds:** low-medium. Most postings ask for 2–5+ years plus samples, and senior roles at AI companies ask for 5+ years ([Cohere listing](https://www.remoterocketship.com/company/cohere/jobs/senior-technical-writer-united-states-remote)). The **Write the Docs job board** is the main specialist channel ([jobs.writethedocs.org](https://jobs.writethedocs.org)).

**Weak points:** no published documentation yet. Portfolio item 4 fixes this.

## 9. AI policy fellowships / policy research

**What's open now:** the GovAI Summer Fellowship 2027, with a deadline of 22 Nov 2026.
- DC track: $24,500 stipend, in person ([GovAI DC](https://www.governance.ai/opportunities/dc-summer-fellowship-2027)).
- London track: £14,000 ([GovAI UK](https://www.governance.ai/post/uk-summer-fellowship-2027-research-track)).

**Watch for:** the RAND AI Policy Research Resident role, which is hybrid with US-remote considered ([listing](https://talents.vaia.com/companies/rand/ai-policy-research-resident-hybrid-remote-120292121/)).

**Odds:** low. These programs are competitive and fixed-term, so they don't fit "steady" well. Their upside is a network and a credential that can lead to steady policy-research work later.

**Weak points:**
- No policy publications.
- Most programs are in person in DC, London or Berkeley.

## 10. Major AI labs (support, solutions, writing)

**Requirements:**
- Anthropic US roles require at least 25% in-office. One Support Operations Specialist posting lists about $131K–$165K ([Built In](https://builtin.com/job/support-operations-specialist/7684402)).
- OpenAI's US-remote customer-facing roles ask for 8+ years ([listing](https://jobgether.com/offer/69725f4b0365b76b0abbd007-ai-success-engineer---us-remote)).

**Odds:** very low this quarter. A few applications cost little. Don't plan around them.

## Roles left out on purpose

- **ML engineer / research scientist:** needs years of engineering or a graduate degree. Your GPU work is a good start but not enough to qualify.
- **Data scientist:** the BLS median is $112,590 ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/)). Postings usually require strong Python/SQL and statistics that you'd need to demonstrate first. It's reachable in a year or more, not in 3–6 months.
- **Market research analyst:** the BLS national median was $76,950 in May 2024 ([BLS](https://bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/market-research-analysts.htm)), and the economics degree fits. It's worth adding to searches as a variant of rank 4. I didn't research specific employers for it.

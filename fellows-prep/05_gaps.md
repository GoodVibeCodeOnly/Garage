# Gaps between this applicant and the bar, stated plainly

The comparison is between the applicant as described (age 30; degree in Business, Economics and History; strong systems thinking and observational work; learning Python; observational rather than lab science) and what the Economics & Policy posting appears to ask for (see `01_program_brief.md`). Where I'm inferring the bar rather than reading it, I say so.

## Gaps that matter most
1. **No research writing sample yet.** The posting asks for "a writing sample, preferably a job-market paper." That's the main artifact, and right now there isn't one. This is the single biggest gap, and the most fixable one.
2. **No research track record.** Many applicants will be PhD students or postdocs in economics or policy with papers, advisors and seminar experience. "Prior experience is a plus but not required" is real, but it means the work sample has to carry the whole case.
3. **Econometrics depth.** *(Inference about the bar.)* Mentors in this track will expect fluency with:
   - panel data
   - fixed effects
   - difference-in-differences and event studies, including the recent literature on staggered adoption
   - clustering and inference
   - the usual threats (pre-trends, selection, measurement error)

   A business, economics and history degree gives intuition but probably not this toolkit at a working level.
4. **Python is still in progress.** The bar is roughly this: load and merge messy public data, run regressions, produce clean figures, and keep a reproducible repo, all without hand-holding. A technical assessment may exist; whether this track has one is unverified. If it does, it would likely test this under time pressure, possibly without AI help, since the Safety track reportedly bans it.
5. **References.** The posting asks for references. Someone who can speak to *research* ability (a professor, a past supervisor on analytical work, or a collaborator on a public project) is worth more than a general character reference. It isn't clear whether such a person exists yet.
6. **No public presence in the field.** The posting asks for a website or LinkedIn. Nothing public yet shows engagement with AI-economics questions: a short post, a replication, an issue filed on the AEI dataset, a comment on a paper.

## Gaps that matter less than they might feel
- **No PhD.** The posting doesn't require one, and the program is explicitly designed to bring people into the field.
- **Age 30, career changer.** I see no sign this counts against anyone. Domain experience and maturity can help in a policy track.
- **"Observational, not lab science."** That's what empirical economics *is*. It's a fit, not a gap.

## Real strengths to lean on
- Systems thinking and close observation are exactly the skills in the first pass that caught the blank-occupation-code bug and the tiny-occupation artifacts. In this field, careful auditing of measurement is undersupplied and valued.
- A history background suits policy diffusion work (Proposal C) and gives a sense for how institutions actually change.
- A preference for real measurement over theory matches the track's empirical emphasis and the AEI's spirit.

## What would close the biggest gaps fastest (options, not a plan)
- **Writing sample:** turn Proposal A's first pass into a finished 10–15 page paper with public code. Roughly 4–8 weeks part-time.
- **Econometrics:**
  - *Mostly Harmless Econometrics* (Angrist & Pischke), or Cunningham's free [*Causal Inference: The Mixtape*](https://mixtape.scunning.com/)
  - Huntington-Klein's free [*The Effect*](https://theeffectbook.net/)

  Use either alongside Proposal B, which forces DiD and event studies.
- **Python under pressure:** practise timed data-wrangling exercises without AI assistance in case the assessment requires it.
- **A smaller first step:** an Economic Futures research award (if open) is a lower-stakes way to build a record with Anthropic's economics team.
- **Timing:** apply now for a possible January 2027 start with a thinner sample, or apply with a finished paper for the next intake. That's genuinely the applicant's call. The rolling Economics & Policy posting appears to allow either (unverified).

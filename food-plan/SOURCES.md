# Sources and how the numbers were made

**The numbers are typical values, not the label on your exact can.** Brands vary. If something matters, the label on the thing in your hand wins.

## Nutrition figures

Protein and fibre for each food are rounded typical values taken from:

- **USDA FoodData Central**: the government's nutrient database. Used for fruit, carrots, milk, chicken meat, eggs, peanuts and popcorn. <https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/>
  - Apple, medium: about 4.4 g fibre. Banana, medium: about 3.1 g fibre. Raspberries, 1 cup: about 8 g. Blueberries, 1 cup: about 3.6 g. Mixed frozen berries are rounded to **5 g a cup**.
  - Roasted chicken meat: about 27 g protein per 100 g. That's ~30 g per 4 oz. **This guide uses 28 g**, because rotisserie chicken carries added broth and salt.
  - Milk: about 8 g protein a cup. Chocolate milk is the same, plus about 1 g fibre from the cocoa.
- **Nutrition Facts labels** on common US products (store brands plus Cheerios, Progresso and Lifeway), rounded:
  - Cheerios: 1½ cups is about 5 g protein and 4 g fibre (current label serving).
  - Canned black beans: ½ cup is about 7 g protein and 7 g fibre, and a 15 oz can holds about 3½ servings. **So half a can is ~11 g protein and ~11 g fibre.** Draining loses a little.
  - Canned chili with beans: 1 cup is about 14 g protein and 7 g fibre. Brands range from 12 to 17 g protein and 5 to 9 g fibre.
  - Canned lentil soup: 1 cup is about 8 g protein and 6 g fibre. A can is about 2 cups.
  - Plain kefir: 1 cup is about 10 g protein.
  - Ready-to-drink protein shake (11 oz): 30 g protein and about 1 g fibre. That's the standard "30 g" store-brand and name-brand type.
  - 100% whole wheat bread: 1 slice is about 4 g protein and 2 g fibre.
  - Deli turkey or ham: 2 oz is about 10 g protein. Beef or pork hot dog: 1 frank is about 5 g protein.
  - Peanut butter: 2 tbsp is about 7 g protein and 2 g fibre.

## Prices

**All prices are estimates.** They're typical store-brand shelf prices at Walmart, Aldi and Food Lion in Durham, NC, as of late 2026. I didn't check them against a live store, so treat them as ballpark.

North Carolina charges no state sales tax on groceries, but it does charge a **2% local tax on food**. The shopping list includes it. Source: NC Department of Revenue, <https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/sales-and-use-tax>

## Water

- **National Academies (2004), *Dietary Reference Intakes for Water…***: adequate intake for adult men is about **3.7 L of total water a day**, counting all drinks and food. About 3 L of that (roughly 13 cups) usually comes from drinks. <https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/10925>

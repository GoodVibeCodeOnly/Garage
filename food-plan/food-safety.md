# Food safety, plainly

**Keep the fridge at 40°F (4°C) or colder and the freezer at 0°F (−18°C).** A $3 fridge thermometer settles it.

## Rotisserie / cooked chicken

- **Get it into the fridge within 2 hours** of buying it, or 1 hour if it's a hot day (over 90°F). Don't leave it on the counter overnight.
- **It's good for 3–4 days in the fridge.** Write the date on the lid with a marker.
- Easiest habit: on day one, pull the meat off the bones into a container or zip bag. It takes 5 minutes, once. The bones go in the trash, and the meat is now grab-and-eat.
- **Won't finish it in 4 days? Freeze half on day one.** It keeps 2–6 months (good quality; it stays safe longer).
- Cold from the fridge is fine to eat. If you reheat it, get it steaming hot all the way through and stir halfway.

## Freezing meat

- At 0°F, frozen food stays **safe indefinitely**. The month limits are only about taste and texture.
- Rough quality limits: **cooked chicken 2–6 months; hot dogs and deli meat 1–2 months.**
- Freeze in flat zip bags, one-serving size, so they thaw fast.
- **Thaw in the fridge overnight, or in the microwave on defrost.** If you microwave-thaw, eat it right away. Don't thaw meat on the counter.
- Deli meat and hot dogs can go in the freezer the day you buy them if you won't get to them.

## Deli meat and hot dogs

- **Deli meat, opened: 3–5 days.** Unopened: about 2 weeks, or the date on the pack.
- **Hot dogs, opened: 1 week.** Unopened: about 2 weeks.
- Heat hot dogs until steaming. 30–45 seconds in the microwave wrapped in a paper towel does it. (USDA says to heat them because they can carry Listeria.)

## Milk, chocolate milk, kefir

- **Opened milk: about 7 days**, kept cold.
- **The date on the carton is a quality date, not a safety cutoff** (USDA FSIS). Milk that's been kept cold is often fine a few days past it. **Sour smell, lumps or an off taste means toss it.**
- **Kefir is supposed to taste sour.** That's normal. Toss it if it grows mould, smells really off or turns fizzy-weird past its date.
- Don't leave milk out longer than 2 hours.
- Milk can be frozen for about 3 months. It gets a bit grainy, so shake it after thawing.

## Opened cans (beans, chili, soup) and shakes

- **Opened cans: move leftovers to a container or cover them. 3–4 days in the fridge.**
- **Protein shakes:** unopened ones keep in the cupboard, and cold ones taste better. After opening, refrigerate and follow the label, usually a day or two.

## Reheating anything

- **Microwave until it's steaming hot all the way through.** Stir halfway, because microwaves leave cold spots.
- **When in doubt, throw it out.** A $1 can isn't worth a bad night.
